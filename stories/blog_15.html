CMMC Level 1 Explained: Why It’s Strictly Pass/Fail and Why Zero POA&Ms Are Allowed

One of the most common misconceptions in defense and federal contracting security is treating CMMC Level 1 like a watered-down version of Level 2 where you can document gaps in a Plan of Action and Milestones (POA&M) and promise to fix them later. Under the federal CMMC rule (32 CFR § 170.21 and FAR 52.204-21), the exact opposite is true: CMMC Level 1 is strictly binary Pass or Fail, and POA&Ms are not permitted under any circumstances.

The Bottom-Line Rule for CMMC Level 1:
To achieve CMMC Level 1 status for a system safeguarding Federal Contract Information (FCI), all 15 basic safeguarding requirements in FAR 52.204-21(b)(1) (which map to 17 security requirements in NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 2 across 6 domains) must be 100% MET at the time of assessment. If even one requirement is Not Met, the entire assessment is a binary FAIL until remediated.

1. What CMMC Level 1 Actually Covers

CMMC Level 1 exists to protect Federal Contract Information (FCI)—information not intended for public release that is provided by or generated for the Government under a contract to develop or deliver a product or service. While Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) triggers the 110 requirements of CMMC Level 2, FCI triggers the foundational safeguarding controls codified in FAR 52.204-21.

Those 15 FAR clauses correspond directly to 17 foundational practices in NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 2 spanning six security domains:

  • Access Control (AC): Limiting system access to authorized users, processes acting on behalf of authorized users, or devices—and limiting those users to the exact types of transactions and functions they are permitted to execute.
  • Identification and Authentication (IA): Identifying system users, processes, and devices, and authenticating those identities before allowing access.
  • Media Protection (MP): Sanitizing or destroying system media containing FCI before disposal or release for reuse.
  • Physical Protection (PE): Limiting physical access to organizational systems, equipment, and operating environments.
  • System and Communications Protection (SC): Monitoring, controlling, and protecting communications at external boundaries and key internal boundaries of the system.
  • System and Information Integrity (SI): Identifying, reporting, and correcting information and system flaws in a timely manner, and protecting against malicious code.

2. Why Zero POA&Ms Are Permitted at Level 1

In many traditional compliance frameworks—and even under specific, restricted conditions in CMMC Level 2—an organization can achieve a conditional status by placing certain lower-weighted controls onto a Plan of Action and Milestones (POA&M) and closing them out within 180 days.

Under 32 CFR § 170.15 and § 170.21, the Department of Defense explicitly prohibited POA&Ms for CMMC Level 1 self-assessments. The reasoning is straightforward: the 15 FAR 52.204-21 practices represent minimum baseline cyber hygiene that federal contractors have already been contractually required to maintain since 2016. Because they are foundational, there is no "partial credit" score at Level 1:

  • 100% of applicable objectives MET: The system achieves PASS and the Affirming Official can submit the annual affirmation.
  • 1 or more objectives NOT MET: The system receives a binary FAIL. The organization cannot submit a Level 1 affirmation until the underlying technical gap is remediated and re-verified.

3. How a Single Operational Gap Triggers a Binary Fail

When you evaluate real production systems against a strict binary standard, abstract policy statements are not enough. You have to look at actual system state, because a single unaddressed anomaly in any of the technical domains becomes a Pre-Affirmation Blocker:

  • Lingering Former Employees or Unreviewed Access (AC & IA): If an access group granting permissions to an FCI service still contains a departed employee, or if an access group is completely unowned and lacks periodic access review governance, you cannot credibly mark Access Control as Met.
  • Human Accounts Inside Machine-Only Service Groups (AC & IA): When a role or group is designated strictly for automated service-to-service communication, finding standing permanent human accounts inside that boundary violates least-privilege separation between human users and non-person entities.
  • Unauthorized Configuration Changes (AC & SC): If production service configurations or security policies can be modified without dual-party review and verified submission provenance, the boundary protecting FCI transactions is not enforced.
  • Overdue Vulnerability Remediation (SI.L1-3.14.1): Requirement 3.14.1 mandates that system flaws are identified, reported, and corrected in a timely manner. If critical or high-severity security vulnerabilities sit open past the organization's defined remediation SLA window, Flaw Remediation fails.

4. Operational Takeaway: Treat Pre-Assessment Checks as Remediation Gates

Because CMMC Level 1 leaves zero room for post-assessment POA&Ms, the only sane way to run a Level 1 program is to treat your internal readiness review as a pre-affirmation engineering gate. Rather than generating a report that tries to explain away a finding, your assessment workflow should surface every single unmet objective as an actionable engineering blocker—so teams can remove the stale account, lock down the service group, or close the overdue vulnerability before the formal self-assessment record is finalized.
