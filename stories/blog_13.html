Vibe Coding at Scale: Shipping Six Full-Stack AI Studios to GitHub in a Single Day

Some days are meant for slow architectural whiteboarding. Today was a day for pure, unapologetic vibe coding and posting apps—locking into a deep creative flow state, orchestrating multi-agent workflows, obsessing over visual craft, and pushing six complete, self-contained AI studios live to GitHub before calling it a night.

Why "Vibe Coding" Still Demands Systems Taste

The phrase vibe coding gets thrown around casually, often implying that you just toss a vague prompt at an LLM and hope whatever comes back compiles. Anyone who has built production software knows that approach collapses the moment you actually open the browser and click around. Canvases fail to resize, headers clip, stock assets look repetitive, and edge cases break the illusion.

To me, real vibe coding is something much more interesting: it's what happens when years of systems engineering instinct, strict quality gates, and modern agentic tooling converge into a high-velocity creative loop. You stay at the director's level—steering the aesthetic, enforcing architectural invariants, and demanding that every canvas, shader, and API endpoint actually feels alive—while eliminating the mechanical friction between having an idea and shipping the repository.

The Six Apps Shipped Today: 1. WonderBook Studio (On-the-fly AI children's picture book creator & 3D reader) • 2. PanelForge (Studio Ghibli & Shonen manga/comic creation studio) • 3. Dungeon Master OS (Autonomous 5e AI Game Master & 2.5D tactical VTT battlemap) • 4. Society in a Bottle (8-agent autonomous village & rumor simulation) • 5. Pantry Chef (Zero-default ingredient-first culinary AI studio) • 6. Eureka Lab (Autonomous ODE physics & symbolic regression workbench).

Sweating the Visual & Interactive Details

The most satisfying part of today's session wasn't just getting six FastAPI backends and SQLite blackboards running across ports 8091 through 8096—it was refusing to settle for "prototype-grade" visuals when testing the boards myself:

  • Dungeon Master OS & Society in a Bottle: A virtual tabletop or a multi-agent town simulation lives and dies by its board. We overhauled both HTML5 Canvas engines so that Dungeon Master OS renders bevelled stone flagstones, flickering radial torchlight sconces, chasm lava hazards, and metallic hero tokens with live HP arcs, while Society in a Bottle paints a lush 60fps 2D village with winding rivers, stone bridges, nine warm-lit buildings, and overhead speech bubbles as citizens gossip and vote.
  • WonderBook Studio (No Two Books Alike): A children's storybook generator shouldn't recycle static illustrations. We engineered a prompt-seeded, multi-layer procedural & neural illustration pipeline so that every single story spread generates unique artwork on the fly—complete with one-click per-page and full-book AI art regeneration.
  • PanelForge (Full-Viewport Studio Ergonomics): When our first pass at the comic creator had top-bar clipping, we refactored the entire workspace into a zero-clip flexbox studio and added an inline Comic Book Creator & AI Artwork Generator bar right at the top—letting you pitch a comic issue and re-roll individual panel artwork in six distinct visual styles on demand.
  • Pantry Chef & Eureka Lab: On the analytical side, Pantry Chef enforces a strict zero-default pantry contract (starting with zero pre-filled ingredients so recipes match your actual kitchen), while Eureka Lab runs Runge-Kutta ODE trajectories through sparse symbolic regression to rediscover governing equations with R² > 0.99.

From Local Ports to Public Repositories

Every single one of these six studios was stress-tested with automated pytest suites and headless Playwright browser inspections before being packaged and pushed to GitHub—expanding the Run Nexus local-first ecosystem to 20+ specialized applications and bringing this portfolio to 27 public systems.

There's a quiet magic to wrapping up a late-night engineering sprint with clean git trees, green test suites, and six brand-new repos live on GitHub. Sometimes the best roadmap is simply sitting down, catching a wave of builder momentum, and shipping until the work speaks for itself.
